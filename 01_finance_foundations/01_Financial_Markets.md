# Financial Markets

## Stocks

A stock represents ownership in a company.

**Example:**  
Buying 10 shares of Apple means owning a small part of Apple.

---

## Bonds

A bond is a loan from an investor to a company or government.

The issuer typically pays interest and returns the principal at maturity.

**Example:**  
You buy a €1,000 government bond paying 3% annual interest. You receive interest and, if held to maturity, the €1,000 principal back.

---

## Interest Rates

The interest rate is the cost of borrowing money and the return for lending it.

**Example:**  
Borrowing €1,000 at 5% for one year means paying approximately €50 in interest.

---

## Maturity

Maturity is the date when a financial contract ends or a bond repays its principal.

**Example:**  
A 10-year bond issued in 2026 matures in 2036.

---

## Yield

Yield is the return an investor earns from an investment, commonly expressed as an annual percentage.

For bonds, yield depends on factors such as the bond's price, coupon and maturity.
**Example:** A bond has a **€1,000 face value**, pays a **€50 annual coupon**, and matures in **5 years**. If you buy it for **€950**, you receive the €50 coupon each year and €1,000 at maturity.

**Example:** You buy a bond for **€950** and receive **€1,000** at maturity → your yield is:

$$
\text{Yield} = \frac{1,000 - 950}{950} = 5.26\%
$$
