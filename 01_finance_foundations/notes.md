# 01 — Finance Foundations

## Financial Markets

- **Stock** → ownership of a company. You earn money if the price rises and/or through dividends.
- **Bond** → a loan you give to a company/government. They pay you interest and return the principal at **maturity**.
- **Interest rate** → the cost of borrowing money / return for lending money.
- **Maturity** → the date when a financial contract ends or a bond repays its principal.
- **Yield** → the return you earn from holding a bond, usually expressed as an annual percentage.

## Derivatives

A **derivative** is a financial contract whose value depends on an underlying asset (stock, bond, interest rate, commodity, etc.).

- **Forward** → agreement to buy/sell an asset in the future at a price agreed today. Usually **OTC**.
- **Future** → like a forward, but standardized and traded on an **exchange**.
- **Option** → gives you the **right, but not the obligation**, to buy/sell an asset at a specified price.
- **Swap** → agreement to exchange cash flows, often used to manage interest-rate or currency risk.
- **Exchange-traded** → standardized contracts traded on an organized exchange.
- **OTC (Over-the-Counter)** → privately negotiated contracts between two parties.

### Forward vs Future

| Forward | Future |
|---|---|
| OTC | Exchange-traded |
| Customized | Standardized |
| Higher counterparty risk | Lower counterparty risk |
| Usually settled at maturity | Usually marked-to-market daily |

## Risk & Return

- **Return** → how much an investment gains or loses.
- **Volatility** → how much returns fluctuate; commonly used as a measure of risk.
- **Risk-free rate** → theoretical return of an investment with negligible default risk.
- **Sharpe ratio** → measures return per unit of risk.

$$
\text{Sharpe Ratio} =
\frac{R_p - R_f}{\sigma_p}
$$

where:

- $R_p$ = portfolio return
- $R_f$ = risk-free rate
- $\sigma_p$ = portfolio volatility

- **Diversification** → combining different assets to reduce unsystematic risk.
